Most teams know they're spending too much time on admin. The hard part is knowing where to start. Automate the wrong thing first and you spend weeks on a project that saves an hour a month. Automate the right thing and your team feels the difference in the first week.

## How to choose what to automate

Score each candidate process on four questions:

1. **How often does it happen?** Daily beats monthly.
2. **Does it follow clear rules?** "If a form comes in, add it to the CRM and send the welcome email" is automatable. "Decide which client deserves a discount" is not.
3. **Does it involve copying information between tools?** Re-typing is the classic sign of a process that wants automating.
4. **What does a mistake cost?** Start where errors are annoying, not dangerous, and add checks before automating anything risky.

## The five processes we usually start with

### 1. Lead follow-up

New enquiries from your website, phone, ads or marketplaces should get a fast, personal response and land in one place. Automation can log every lead in your CRM, reply within minutes with the right information, notify the right person, and follow up if nobody responds.

### 2. Appointment reminders and confirmations

Reminders by text or email, easy rescheduling links, and a follow-up after the appointment (a thank-you, a review request, or the next service due). This reduces no-shows and keeps your calendar accurate.

### 3. Data entry between systems

If someone copies orders from email into a spreadsheet, or customer details from a form into your accounting software, that's a strong candidate. Connecting the tools directly saves time and removes typos.

### 4. Invoicing and payment reminders

Create invoices from completed jobs, send them automatically, and send polite reminders when they're overdue. Your team only steps in for the exceptions.

### 5. Internal reporting

The weekly numbers someone assembles by hand from three systems can usually be collected automatically and sent as a short summary, with alerts when something looks off.

## Common mistakes

- **Automating a broken process.** Simplify first. Remove steps nobody needs before you automate the ones that remain.
- **No owner.** Every automation needs a named person who gets alerted when it fails.
- **No exceptions path.** Real life doesn't follow the happy path. Decide what happens when data is missing or something looks wrong.
- **Too many tools.** Prefer connecting the tools you already use over adding another platform.

## Which automation platform?

Zapier, Make and n8n are all capable. The right choice depends on how complex your workflows are, whether you need to self-host for data reasons, and who will maintain it. For many businesses the bigger decision isn't the platform, it's which processes to automate and how to monitor them.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a first automation take to build?

Simple, well-defined automations can be live within days. Projects that connect several systems or need careful testing usually take a few weeks.

### Will automation replace my staff?

It usually replaces tasks, not people. The goal is to give your team back the hours they spend re-typing and chasing, so they can do the work that needs a person.

### What should never be fully automated?

Decisions that need judgement or carry real risk, such as refunds above a threshold, legal or medical advice, or anything a customer is upset about. Automate the preparation, keep a person on the decision.
