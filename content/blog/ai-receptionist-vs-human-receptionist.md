If you run a clinic, a shop, a salon or a service department, the phone is probably both your best source of business and your biggest interruption. AI receptionists promise to fix that. Some of the promises are real, and some aren't. Here's a straight comparison.

## What an AI receptionist does well

- **Answers every call, at any hour.** No voicemail, no hold music, no "we're closed".
- **Handles several calls at once.** The lunch rush doesn't create a queue.
- **Is consistent.** It gives the same accurate answer about hours, prices and policies every time, as long as that information is kept up to date.
- **Books directly** into your calendar or scheduler and sends a confirmation text.
- **Writes things down.** Every call gets a summary, so nothing depends on someone remembering to pass on a message.

## What a person still does better

- **Judgement calls.** Unusual requests, exceptions to policy, and anything where the right answer is "let me check with the owner".
- **Difficult conversations.** An upset customer, a billing dispute, bad news about a repair.
- **Relationships.** Regular customers who like to chat with someone they know.
- **Handling the unexpected.** AI is good at the routine; people are better at the genuinely new.

## The comparison, side by side

| | AI receptionist | Human receptionist |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | 24/7, including holidays | Business hours, plus cover for breaks and absences |
| Simultaneous calls | Many | One at a time |
| Consistency | Same answer every time | Varies with experience and workload |
| Judgement and empathy | Limited; should hand off | Strong |
| Setup | Needs your information, rules and integrations | Needs hiring and training |
| Ongoing work | Keep information current; review calls | Management, scheduling, turnover |

## Costs to compare honestly

For a person, count wages, benefits, cover for breaks, sick days and holidays, training, and turnover. For an AI receptionist, count setup, the monthly plan, phone and usage costs, and the time someone on your team spends keeping it current and reviewing calls. Many businesses find the real comparison isn't AI versus a full-time hire, but AI versus the calls nobody answers today.

## How most businesses use both

The setups that work best don't replace the front desk; they protect it:

1. **After hours and weekends:** the AI answers and books.
2. **Overflow:** when the front desk is busy, calls roll to the AI instead of voicemail.
3. **Routine calls:** booking, rescheduling, hours, directions and status checks.
4. **Hand-off:** anything sensitive or unusual goes to a person, with a summary so the customer doesn't repeat themselves.

## Questions to ask before you buy

- What exactly can it do in *my* calendar or booking system?
- Can I control what it says, and what it won't discuss?
- How does it hand off to a person, and what happens after hours?
- Where are recordings and transcripts stored, and who can see them?
- Will I see every call and its outcome?

## Frequently asked questions

### Do customers mind talking to an AI receptionist?

Most customers care about being answered quickly and getting what they called for. Being upfront that it's an AI, and making it easy to reach a person, matters more than trying to sound human.

### Can an AI receptionist take payments?

It can, through a proper payment system, but for most small businesses it's better to keep card details out of voice calls entirely and send a secure payment link instead.

### What happens if the AI doesn't know the answer?

A well-configured receptionist says so, takes a message or transfers the call, and flags it for your team. It should never guess about prices or policies.
